---
title: Roles
description: How roles represent responsibilities and govern access to work in Process Focus.
---

A **role** represents a responsibility in your organisation. Assign roles to
users so they can do the work that goes with those responsibilities. Several
users can share a role, and one user can have several roles.

## Where roles belong

Define roles for shared responsibilities at the [organisation](/docs/concepts/organisation/)
level, and roles for a particular department or team in that organisational
unit. For example, define an **Employee** role at the organisation level and an
**Accounts Manager** role in the Accounts department. Someone who manages
Accounts can hold both roles.

Defining a role in a unit does not give it to everyone in that unit. Role
assignments are separate from where the role is defined.

## How roles affect access

A role's name and place in the organisation do not grant permissions by
themselves. [Authorisation](/docs/concepts/authorisation/) determines what users
with that role can do.

For example, in a reimbursement process, you can set the submission step's
role to **Employee** and the approval step's role to **Accounts Manager**. Under
the default policies, the Employee role lets a user submit a claim but does
not let them approve one.

See the [Roles reference](/docs/reference/authorisation/roles/) for how to define
and assign roles, or [Assigning permissions to roles](/docs/reference/authorisation/role-permissions/)
for how to use them in processes and policies.
